The Art of Shift-Left Testing

Prevent the defect in the requirements instead of detecting it after implementation. How QA refines the Acceptance Criteria before the sprint, and why every project needs it.

Select → Refine → Hand offShift-Left · QA enters at the backlog, not at the end
Jira · UPEX standard · /shift-left-testing
Framing: shift-left moves QA to the left of the cycle, into the requirements phase. It refines the ACs of backlog Stories, uncovers ambiguities and gaps, and hands them to PO/Dev BEFORE the sprint. Two pillars of the deck: HOW to do it well and WHY to do it in every project.

the economics of a defect

1 → 10 → 100

Fixing a defect costs 1× in the requirements, ~10× in development and ~100× in production. Shift-left pushes quality to where the defect is cheapest to kill: before a single line is written.

The defect cost curve (Boehm / IBM System Science Institute): the cost of fixing grows ~10x per phase. The thesis of shift-left is to move detection to the left. We come back to the number on the curve slide.

why · the thesis

Prevent, Don't Detect

Traditional QA detects defects after the build. Shift-left prevents them where they are born: the requirements. An ambiguous AC is a defect waiting to happen.

❌ QA at the end (detect)

  • Ambiguous AC: the dev builds the wrong thing
  • The bug is found in QA or in production
  • Rework: re-design + re-code + re-test
  • The defect already cost 10× or 100×
←

✓ QA at the start (prevent)

  • QA refines the AC before the sprint
  • The ambiguity is settled as a question to the PO
  • The dev builds the right thing the first time
  • The defect never existed: cost 1×
The name says it: shift LEFT = move QA to the left of the timeline (requirements), not to the right (post-build). The skill's canonical phrase: "defects prevented in the requirements, not detected after implementation".

why · the economics

The cost of fixing a defect grows exponentially with every phase it crosses undetected. Shift-left flips the bet: it spends hours of refinement in the 1× phase so it doesn't spend weeks in the 100× phase.

Classic research (Boehm, IBM System Science Institute, NIST): the relative cost of fixing grows ~10x per phase. Pre-sprint refinement is the cheapest investment in quality there is.

why · in every project

What Shift-Left Prevents, in Every Project

Ambiguity → rework

An AC that allows two readings: the dev picks the wrong one and the work is redesigned. Settled as a question to the PO before the Story reaches Ready For Dev.

Gap → defect in prod

A negative path with no AC: nobody builds it or tests it, and it fails in production. Raised as a gap before the sprint.

Untestable AC → false positive

"Shows an error" with no exact text: QA writes an assert that passes with the wrong error. Made measurable before coding.

And an extra benefit: exposing mislabeled work

While filtering candidates, shift-left reveals that what looked like a Story is really a Bug or a misclassified Tech-debt item. Surfacing is the value: even a clean Story comes out with an empty question list, and that is also a valid result.

Three classes of defect that shift-left prevents in any project: ambiguity (rework), gap (prod defect), untestable AC (QA false positive). That is why it applies to EVERY project, not just the big ones.

PART 01

The Shift-Left
Pipeline, Before the Sprint

Three phases, always in order: Selection → Refinement → Handoff. Batch by design: one session refines N Stories for a single grooming pass with the team.

Shift-Left is the first stage of the QA pipeline (historically Stage 0). It enters at the backlog and exits at Ready For Dev. Batch of 1-12 Stories.

three phases, in order

Selection → Refinement → Handoff

1

Selection

Filters candidates from the backlog (Stories only), applies veto + risk score, presents a ranked table. WAIT for the user's OK.

2

Refinement (the core)

Per Story: ambiguities + gaps + unwritten edge cases + testability. Refines ACs (Given/When/Then) and sketches outlines. No execution.

3

Handoff

Writes to Jira: refined ACs, pre-sprint ATP (outline scope), questions for PO/Dev, labels, AI Effort, transition backlog → ready_for_dev. Batch report.

Batch by design

There is no single-issue mode: one session refines N Stories (sweet spot 1-12) so the PO + Dev lead can run a single grooming pass with the team. >12: split into sessions.

Selection → Refinement → Handoff, always in that order. Every phase has a user gate. Batch because shift-left happens in team grooming.

Deliberate reuse

Most of the refinement logic already lives in acceptance-test-planning.md. Shift-left cites it and doesn't duplicate it, and the work done here is reused in-sprint (not repeated).

Same base methodology, different moment and depth. Shift-left doesn't execute (the feature doesn't exist). The output feeds the AI Effort and the decision to split.

what it does NOT do

The Limits of the Scope

  • Stories only: Bugs, Spikes, Sub-tasks, Tech-debt are rejected. A bug is reactive and has no upstream ACs to refine.
  • Refines, doesn't execute: no smoke, no DB queries, no API calls. Feasibility is established by reading code + APIs + schema.
  • Stops at Ready For Dev: it never goes further. The Story leaves with its AI Effort assigned and the PO decides whether to split it.
  • No code, no commit: Jira is canonical. shift-left-refinement.md is a gitignored working artifact.
  • No test data, no parameterization: outline names only. That is deferred to in-sprint.
  • No TCs pre-sprint: outline names only. The Test item is created by /sprint-testing Planning (Xray) or /test-documentation (jira-native), depending on testing.tc_creation_stage.

The line with sprint-testing

Everything that requires running the system (data generation, parameterization, fixtures) belongs to /sprint-testing Planning. Shift-left is doc-only.

Clear limits: Stories only, no execution, stops at Ready For Dev, no code. The feature doesn't exist yet, which is why there is no data/fixtures/TCs.

PART 02

Selection
Which Story to Refine

Not every Story deserves refinement. The veto discards the trivial; the risk score sets the depth. The goal: spend the attention budget where the risk is real.

Phase 1. Veto beats risk score. Selection decides what enters the loop and at what depth.

what qualifies

Backlog Candidates

JQL · candidates (example statuses: the real ones are in .agents/jira-workflows.json)
project = {{PROJECT_KEY}} AND issueType = Story
AND status in (Backlog, "Shift-Left QA", "Ready For Dev")
ORDER BY priority DESC, created DESC

# optional filters:
AND sprint in openSprints()      # candidates for the next sprint
AND labels != "shift-left-reviewed"  # (but PREFER to surface them)

Type filter (hard)

Only Story. Bug/Spike/Sub-task/Tech-debt: rejected, with a one-line reason. Never a silent drop.

Label freshness

shift-left-reviewed <30 days: already refined (surface it: refresh?). >30 days: refine again (it may have drifted).

Entry: the JQL statuses (backlog/shift-left-qa/ready-for-dev in the example project). Stories only. Already-reviewed ones are surfaced, not auto-skipped.

the veto beats the score

Veto: Skip vs Force

SKIP (discard)

  • Backend-only with no UI or contract change
  • Pure CSS / styling
  • Static copy / documentation
  • Tech-debt with no behavior change
  • DB-only with no business logic
vs

FORCE FULL (force)

  • Money / billing / refund / conversion
  • Data integrity on core entities
  • Auth / authorization / session / RBAC
  • External integrations (Stripe, Auth0…)
  • State machines · calculations / formulas

The veto beats the risk score

A money or auth Story is refined in full whatever its score. The risk of a wrong calculation or an auth bypass is not negotiated against a number.

Veto beats score. The trivial is discarded; the critical (money/data/auth/integrations/states/calculations) is forced to full refinement.

if there is no veto: set the depth

Risk Score → Depth

Factors (they add up)

  • +3 new feature · +3 dynamic data (API/DB)
  • +2 explicit ACs · +2 user-facing · +2 high effort
  • +2 AC-light (<2 ACs or one-liners): more refinement
  • +1 high priority · multi-component · external dependency
  • +1 >5 comments: unresolved disagreement

Verdict

0-3 LOW
SKIP: PO/Dev write the ACs without QA
4-7 MEDIUM
Standard refinement
8+ HIGH
Extended + edge-case scan + data feasibility
The score sets the depth. AC-light (+2) is a signal that this very Story needs MORE refinement. HIGH adds a data feasibility scan.

quiz · selection

Refine or Skip?

Q1A tax calculation Story has a risk score of 2 (LOW). What do you do?

FORCE FULL. The veto beats the risk score: money, data, auth, integrations, state machines and calculations/formulas are refined in full whatever the score. Tax precision is not negotiable.
Q2The user pastes 5 IDs to refine; one is a Bug, "the button doesn't respond". What happens to the Bug?

BIt is rejected with a one-line reason: shift-left is Stories only

Rejected (visibly). Hard type filter: Stories only. A bug is reactive and has no upstream ACs to refine. It is surfaced with a reason, never silently dropped. Surfacing the mislabeled work IS part of the value.
Veto beats score (tax → full). Stories only (bug → visible rejection). Two core rules of selection.

PART 03

Refinement
The Core of Shift-Left

Here is the value: uncovering the ambiguities, gaps and edge cases the Story stays quiet about. The ACs are the floor, and refinement pushes past the happy path.

Phase 2, the core. Applied DEEPER than in-sprint because the PO can still act. ACs = floor, not ceiling.

how to refine well

The Five Sub-Analyses of the Core

1 · Ambiguities

Text that allows several readings: location + question for the PO + testing impact + suggested clarification.

2 · Gaps (missing info)

Type (AC / technical detail / business rule) + why it is critical + what to add + risk if omitted.

3 · Unwritten edge cases

Scenario + expected behavior (best guess) + criticality + action. Marked NEEDS PO/DEV CONFIRMATION.

4 · Testability

Yes / Partial / No + list of issues (vague AC, no error text, no sample data, no perf criterion, not isolatable).

5 · Contradictions

Sections of the Story that contradict each other. Surface it explicitly: "The description says X but AC3 says Y".

The 5 sub-analyses ARE the gap taxonomy that shift-left uncovers. #3 (edge cases) and #5 (contradictions) are where QA adds what the PO didn't see.

what makes an AC good

Testability: the Failure Modes

UNtestable AC

  • Vague: "works fast" (no metric)
  • No error text: "shows an error"
  • No sample data
  • No performance criterion
  • Not isolatable: depends on another feature

Testable AC

  • Measurable: "responds in < 2 seconds"
  • Exact error: text + status code
  • With concrete data
  • With a perf budget (p95)
  • Isolatable: verifiable on its own

The cost of a vague AC

"Shows an error" with no exact text: QA writes a "shows some error" assert that passes with the wrong error. A false positive in QA plus a defect in production. That is why testability is validated BEFORE coding.

The 5 testability failure modes (verbatim from the skill). The "shows an error" → false positive example is the canonical case of why it matters.

the ACs are the floor, not the ceiling

Explode 1:N: Push Past the Happy Path

A non-trivial AC implies several scenarios. Refinement derives them from the shape of the AC and tags each gap with its technique.

If the AC has…TechniqueDerives
a range / limitBoundary Value Analysismin-1·min·min+1 … max-1·max·max+1
a state fieldState-Transition1 per valid transition + 1 per invalid one
2+ interacting conditionsDecision Table1 per surviving rule
3+ combinable factorsPairwiseall-pairs set (log of the reduction)

Collapsing to 1 scenario requires a justification

"Trivially atomic" (a boolean, with no ranges/states/interactions), and it is said in writing. A one-line AC that hides a range or a state is NOT a 1-scenario AC.

Connects with the techniques of the test-case-craft deck. Refinement tags each gap with its technique. 1:N is the default; collapsing requires justification.

the refiner's tool

The Question Bank: a Rubric, not a Script

1

Identify the archetype

Auth · Money · Search · State Machine · Integration · CRUD · Notification · RBAC · Performance · a11y/i18n. Each Story touches 2-3.

2

Walk the archetype's list

Every question with no explicit answer in the Story is a gap candidate.

3

Classify the gap

Critical: blocks sprint planning (question for the PO) · Important: blocks implementation (for Dev) · Edge: testable, doesn't block.

A gap is not just a question, it is also a missing outline

And each archetype maps to its technique: State Machine → State-Transition; Money/ranges → BVA; RBAC → Decision Table; multi-factor configs → Pairwise.

The bank is a rubric (not a script): if the Story already answers, it isn't asked again. Classifying well (Critical/Important/Edge) is a sign of judgment. Each gap = question + outline.

from gap to actionable AC

Refined ACs: Given / When / Then

Every refined scenario carries a Type (Positive/Negative/Boundary/Edge) + Priority + Given/When/Then with exact values: UI + API (status+body) + DB + system state.

derived outlines: "Should <behavior> <condition>"
Should redirect to dashboard after successful OTP entry with valid code
Should display "Code expired" error after OTP entry with code older than 5 min
Should reject negative refund amount on POST /refunds
# inferred, not literal in the Story:
Should show "Session expired" when OTP submitted after session expiry   NEEDS PO/DEV CONFIRMATION

The marker that survives Jira

Every scenario that refinement inferred (it wasn't literal in the Story) carries NEEDS PO/DEV CONFIRMATION, verbatim, in English. The marker travels to the Jira comment so the PO sees it in sprint planning.

The refined AC (Given/When/Then) is the business assertion; the outline (Should…) is its exploration. What is inferred ALWAYS carries the literal marker NEEDS PO/DEV CONFIRMATION (tooling greps for it).

quality > quantity

The Art of NOT Asking

The scarcest resource in a grooming session is the team's attention. A redundant question trains the team to ignore QA's future output.

  • Clear Story: say so. "Story quality: good, no significant gaps" beats inventing questions. An empty list is a valid result.
  • A rubric, not a script. If the Story is already explicit on a topic, don't ask again.
  • Cite the context instead of asking. If the data map (business-data-context) already defines the currency or the state machine, or the glossary (business-domain-context) already defines the term, cite it. The PO has finite patience.
  • Classify with judgment. Putting a success-text question next to an auth-bypass one signals a lack of judgment.
  • When you do need to ask, ask well. With the PO in the room, the grilling skill runs them in rounds, each with its recommended answer. If the PO answers later, to-questionnaire gathers the Critical ones into a single questionnaire.

Anti-pattern L1 (the most important)

NEVER force questions to fill a checklist. Shift-left adds value by uncovering real risk, not by inflating question counts.

The user values this: rule #4 of AGENTS.md. Don't force questions. Quality over quantity. A clean Story leaves with an empty list and that is fine.

PART 04

Artifacts + Handoff
Delivering to PO and Dev

The pre-sprint ATP makes the effort of verifying the Story visible. The handoff moves it to Ready For Dev with labels that let the sprint reuse the work instead of repeating it.

Phase 3. The output lands in Jira: refined ACs, pre-sprint ATP, questions, labels, transition. And the batch report.

the central artifact

A single ATP: born as an outline, matured into an executable

Sections

  • Phase 1: Critical Analysis (context + complexity)
  • Phase 2: Quality (ambiguities, gaps, edge, testability)
  • Phase 3: Refined ACs (Given/When/Then)
  • Phase 4: Outlines (NAMES) + coverage count
  • Critical Questions for PO · Tech Questions for Dev
  • Suggested Improvements · Data feasibility

Outline maturity, not another artifact

  • NO parameterization tables
  • NO test-data JSON per outline
  • NO numbered steps or Faker
  • NO TC creation
  • YES coverage count (feeds the AI Effort)
  • YES outline names + a one-line precondition

Status: "Refined — Ready For Dev"

The ATP is one and real from day zero: pre-sprint it lives ONLY in the acceptance_test_plan field (field-first), and shift-left-refinement.md is just the local staging buffer. What changes later is not the identity but the maturity: pre-sprint, an outline (and that is already enough to judge the AI Effort); in sprint, /sprint-testing Planning creates the Test Plan item "ATP: {STORY-KEY}: {title}" FROM that field and adds what was deferred (data, parameterization, steps) in the executable superset.

The two TMS modalities

This applies equally to jira-native (the default modality) and jira-xray: neither creates the Test Plan item at this stage. Pre-sprint only the acceptance_test_plan field exists.

One ATP per Story, field-first: shift-left authors it early at outline maturity inside the acceptance_test_plan field (names + coverage, no test data/parameterization/TCs); the Test Plan item is born in Planning from that field, refined into the executable superset. shift-left-refinement.md is only the local buffer; the shift-left-reviewed + date labels mark the pre-sprint pass. The coverage count DOES go in (it feeds the AI Effort).

the state machine

The Handoff: Backlog → Ready For Dev

Backlog — analyze → Shift-Left QA — refined → Ready For Dev ✓ ⊘ In Progress

Writes to Jira

Refined ACs to the field · pre-sprint ATP to the acceptance_test_plan field (the Test Plan item is born in Planning) · "QA Refinements" section in the description (append, never overwrite).

Notifies

1 handoff comment with @PO / @Dev (only if the handle is in project.yaml).

Transitions

analyze → refined. If it already went past ready_for_dev: log a warning + SKIP (the refinement still lands). The [QA] Shift-Left Review subtask (in ACTIVE since acceptance in Phase 1) goes to Close with the complete transition.

Guardrail: NEVER past Ready For Dev

Shift-left stops at Ready For Dev with the AI Effort assigned (Low, Medium or High): the effort to implement and verify the whole Story with agents, not a time or a token count. High means it crosses layers, touches auth, money or data, can't verify itself, or doesn't fit in one sentence: QA leaves a note that it may need splitting; that is only a warning, the Story still goes to Ready For Dev and splitting it is optional. The cost moved from building to verifying, which is why the effort of verifying is what gets labeled.

State machine: backlog→(analyze)→shift_left_qa→(refined)→ready_for_dev. Hard guardrail: never past ready_for_dev. Tokens are not estimated: Development Token Usage (K) and Quality Token Usage (K) are optional, measured afterwards if the harness shows them (empty is valid), and the sprint is read with the previous sprint's averages. The description is appended to, never overwritten.

work done once, reused

The Labels and the Short-Circuit

shift-left-reviewed

The soft marker. It says the pre-sprint refinement happened, and /sprint-testing Planning reads it as a claim, not as evidence.

shift-left-{YYYY-MM-DD}

The freshness marker. After more than 30 days the refinement counts as stale and the full flow runs.

→

When the Story reaches Ready For QA…

/sprint-testing starts from the full flow. It skips Phases 1-3 only if it cites the evidence: both labels under 30 days old, the ATP body published in Jira (the synced acceptance-test-plan.md) and the refined ACs still identical to the description. Then it adds what was deferred (data, parameterization, steps) as a superset. A label without an ATP opens nothing.

This is the economics of shift-left

Refinement is done once, pre-sprint, and reused in-sprint, not repeated. The quality cost is paid in the 1× phase, not the 10× one.

Two labels: reviewed (soft marker) + dated (freshness). Alone they are not enough: the gate is fail-closed and opens with the ATP published in Jira as evidence (sprint-testing/references/acceptance-test-planning.md). That reuse IS the economic payoff of shift-left.

never do this

Shift-Left Anti-Patterns

  • L1 · Don't force questions to fill a checklist. Clean Story = valid empty list.
  • L2 · Don't skip the shift-left-reviewed label when transitioning: it breaks the short-circuit.
  • L3 · Don't mix bugs with Stories in the batch. Stories only.
  • L5 · Don't transition to Ready For Dev without the pre-sprint ATP filled in: without it, the AI Effort is assigned blind.
  • L4 · Don't write ADF by hand: local Markdown, let the md-to-ADF conversion do its job.
  • L6 · Don't refine >12 Stories per session: quality and attention collapse.
  • L7 · Don't ask what the AC already answers: the reader's bandwidth is the scarcest resource.
  • Guardrail · Don't go past Ready For Dev: the PO decides whether to split and what enters the sprint.
The anti-patterns L1-L7 + the guardrail. L1 (don't force questions) and L5 (ATP filled in before moving to Ready For Dev) are the most load-bearing.

the refiner's checklist

How to Do It Well

  • Veto before score: money/auth/data/integrations are forced to full.
  • Walk the archetypes, don't ask at random: each Story touches 2-3.
  • Cite the context (maps and glossary) instead of asking what is already documented; a new term is proposed to business-domain-context.
  • Tag each gap with its technique (BVA/State/Decision/Pairwise).
  • Mark what is inferred with NEEDS PO/DEV CONFIRMATION.
  • Classify with judgment: Critical (blocks the PO) vs Edge (doesn't block).
  • Include the coverage count: it feeds the AI Effort.
  • Quality > quantity: one real question is worth more than ten filler ones.

The goal

PO + Dev arrive at sprint planning with unambiguous ACs, visible risks and an assigned AI Effort, so Dev builds the right thing the first time.

The operational checklist for HOW to do it well. Every point is a quality lever of refinement.

It's not just for big projects

Any project with requirements has ambiguities, gaps and untestable ACs. Shift-left turns them into cheap questions before they become expensive defects. The ROI is structural: hours of refinement vs weeks of rework.

takeaways

Three Ideas That Stay

1

Prevent, don't detect

QA enters at the requirements, not at the end. An ambiguous AC is a defect waiting to happen. Cost 1× vs 100×.

2

The core is refinement

Ambiguities, gaps, edge cases, testability. The question bank as a rubric: quality > quantity.

3

A handoff that gets reused

An estimable pre-sprint ATP + labels that let the sprint skip work already done. Once, not twice.

The three core concepts: prevention (why), refinement (how), reusable handoff (the economics).

Move Quality to the Left.

Select by risk → refine the core (ambiguities, gaps, the question bank) → hand off a pre-sprint ATP with the AI Effort assigned, which the sprint refines and doesn't repeat. Prevent the defect where it costs 1×, not where it costs 100×.
